# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users
Internal Blessing Computers staff operating the business day to day, under dynamic
role-based access control. Distinct roles: sales agents (process customer orders and
quotes, handle outsourced-supplier approvals), admins (manage catalog, inventory,
pricing, invoices), social-media managers (content), and a super admin (governs RBAC
and system configuration). Their context is repeated, high-volume operational work,
often under time pressure. The primary job on any screen is to move a task forward
quickly and correctly — fulfill an order, update stock, reconcile an invoice, resolve
a customer chat — with the current state legible at a glance.

## Product Purpose
The operations cockpit behind the storefront: the tool staff use to run orders,
catalog, inventory, invoicing, customer communication, and access governance. It
serves the work — success is measured in throughput, accuracy, and low friction, not
in visual spectacle. It exists so the humans who stand behind every customer order can
do that job well.

## Positioning
Expert-backed tech retail — real people behind every order. In the admin, that promise
turns inward: the tool must let staff deliver the genuine, well-supported service the
storefront promises customers.

## Brand Personality
Trustworthy, expert, premium — carried in service of the work. It shares the
storefront's maroon/navy/gold identity but spends it on clarity and focus, not
decoration. Confident and calm; an operator should feel the tool is competent and in
control, never toy-like or noisy.

## Anti-references
Explicitly NOT:
- A cold enterprise or government admin — sterile gray dashboards, dense forms with no
  hierarchy, no brand presence.
- A generic template SaaS admin — interchangeable card-grid dashboards, gradient
  accents, hero-metric clichés.
- A cluttered, noisy back-office — competing colors and controls that bury the one
  action that matters.
- Flashy gamer/RGB styling — inappropriate for a focused work tool.

## Design Principles
- Operators move fast. Optimize for repeated, high-volume tasks — reduce clicks and
  cognitive load; the common path should be the shortest path.
- Clarity under complexity. Orders, RBAC, and invoices carry real complexity; make
  state (status, ownership, what needs action) legible at a glance.
- Same brand, in service of work. Use the shared maroon/gold identity for focus and
  trust, not spectacle — gold stays a deliberate accent, not decoration.
- Support the humans. Staff are the real people behind every order; the tool should
  make good, fast customer service easy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG 2.1 AA: ≥4.5:1 body-text contrast (including on the cool-gray canvas and in
status badges), full keyboard operability for data-dense tables and forms, visible
focus states, and reduced-motion alternatives. Status and state must never rely on
color alone — pair with text or icon.
